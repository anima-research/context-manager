- `branchAt(messageId)` finds a row of an auxiliary slot too, where it threw `Message not found` before (agent-framework #291). Once a host presents rows on the branch (`presentRows`), a row is in the window only from the write that presented it, so for a row the window holds the branch is cut at that write, the last of its body's shards, rather than at the row's own sequence. The new branch's window then holds the row and every row presented before it. Rows stored after the row and not presented by then are in the new branch's record, arrived and not presented. A row the window doesn't hold, and every row in legacy mode, is cut at its own sequence, as before. So is a row the window holds from its write-down (the first `presentRows` on the branch), which was in the window from its own write: the new branch is in legacy mode, and its window is the record up to the row, as a branch made before the write-down would have it.
- `derive({ atSequence })` cuts at the sequence given. Once a host presents rows, a row enters the window at a write after its own, so a cut at a presented row's own sequence holds the row in the child's record but not in its window: the child lists it among its arrived rows (`getUnpresentedMessages`). Give `atSequence` as a presentation boundary, such as `currentSequence()` read after a `presentRows`. Hosts that pass a row's own sequence, such as agent-framework's `inspectAgentContext({ atSequence })` and `inherit.atSequence` callers, need to change before they present.
