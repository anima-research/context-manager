- `ContextStrategy.onRetracted(rows, ctx, { undone, removed, ids })`: the strategy hears rows as they leave the agent's window (agent-framework #291), with the rows that left, in the order the window held them, every shard of a body, and a context whose view no longer holds them (except where a `presentRows` moves a body whole, below: the move is one write, so the view holds the shards that moved at their new places). `ids` names every row that left in the call, in the same order, and `rows` gives the ones the manager can give: a row the store holds, or one a removal through this manager read before it went. A row removed through another manager, which this one doesn't hear, has no row to give: it comes by its id alone, with `removed: true`, and `rows` can be empty, so a strategy that keeps rows by id lets go of it all the same.
  - `retractRows` calls it once per call, after the rows are out and before it returns, with `undone` as given and `removed: false`; the rows the store no longer holds, which it takes out by their ids, are told in a second call of their own, by `ids` alone, with `removed: true`. `retractRemoved` and `retractAbsentRows` call it by `ids` alone, with `removed: true`. Once a host presents rows on a branch, a removal through the manager (`removeMessage`, `removeMessages`) of rows the window held calls it with the rows as they were before they went, `removed: true`, whether or not the window's tombstone could be written, since the removal stands; a row whose tombstone failed is told again, by its id, when `retractAbsentRows` takes it out. Where the window index can't be read to tell which rows it held, or whether it is in legacy mode, a removal through the manager calls it with every row removed, in the order given and as they were before they went, with `removed: true`, and the rows keep their ordinals until `retractAbsentRows` takes them out, which tells the strategy again. A sharded `addMessage` that fails partway takes a shard a host's `onMessage` listener presented out of the window as it removes the shards it stored, and calls it, with `removed: true`, as `removeMessages` does. A `presentRows` that puts a body back whole, its first shard out and later ones in, calls it for those later shards before `onPresented` hears the body; where that call's hook moves the store's branch, sets a strategy, starts an initialization or closes the manager, the body waits, as rows a hook stopped do, and is heard once the strategy is initialized, where the window of the branch it initialized on holds it. `retractRows`' second call, for rows the store no longer holds, waits the same way after such a hook of its first. In legacy mode a removal calls nothing: the window is the record, and a strategy that folded the row keeps what it folded, as before.
  - Fire and forget, as `onPresented` (#288): a throw or a rejection is logged as `Strategy onRetracted failed for …`, naming the rows, and the rows stay out. A call's tombstones are one record (see the window index entry), so a write the store refuses takes nothing out and tells the strategy nothing. While the strategy initializes, the calls wait and are made in order once it has, before the rows presented meanwhile are heard; they wait for the manager's strategy, whether or not the one initializing has the hook, so one set with `setStrategy()` meanwhile is told; a row presented again since is still given, and a row the initialized branch holds at the ordinal it left, in its window and its record (a branch made before it left), or any row while that branch is in legacy mode, is left out.
  - `AutobiographicalStrategy` (and `KnowledgeStrategy`) let go of what covers a row that left, every row `ids` names: each L1 with the row among its sources is dropped, with every summary above it, and a summary the drop leaves without its parent renders and merges again; each chunk record holding the row gives it up and is compressed again over the rows it keeps (one left with none is removed). The summaries, chunk records and merge queue are written one after another, the summaries first, with one `[autobiographical] … row(s) left the window` warning naming what changed. Should the process stop between those writes, the next open repairs what is left: a chunk record whose L1 is gone is compressed again, a queued merge of a dropped summary goes, and, once a host presents, under `topologyPolicy` `'reject'`, a record whose rows the window holds apart is trimmed to its longest run, its other rows chunked again where the window holds them, with one `[autobiographical] the window holds the rows of … uncompressed chunk record(s) apart` warning, so it is not compressed across the window. A compression or merge in flight over the row writes nothing when it lands, also in another strategy instance sharing the store, which doesn't hear the row go: a merge reads the store's summaries before its call and again before it writes, and one with a source the store no longer holds writes nothing (found before the call, it makes none); an L1 reads its chunk record before it writes, and writes nothing unless the store still holds the record uncompressed, over the rows it held when the compression began. Either then takes up the store's state, with an `executeMerge: … source(s) dropped …` (found before the call, `… source(s) missing from the store (dropped by another instance sharing it, or a write of this one's that failed) …`) or `discarding L1: its chunk record … changed in the store mid-request` warning. So an undone turn's rows no longer render folded after the undo, and a redo, which presents them again at the end of the window, renders each once, raw there until it is folded again, where it rendered twice before (raw, and inside the summary at its old place). A removal through one manager unfolds the row on every reader the host repairs with `retractRemoved` or `retractAbsentRows`, not only on the manager that removed it. The cost is the fold work over those rows, on each reader: the summaries above them are written again. Under `autoTickOnNewMessage` it starts background compression from `onRetracted` too, as from `onPresented`, so a retraction, or a removal once a host presents, can start model calls. In legacy mode nothing changes. `PassthroughStrategy` and `WindowedPassthroughStrategy` define no `onRetracted`.
  - A row that left and was presented again before the strategy heard it go (both while it initialized) is in the window again when it is told: what covered it at its old place goes all the same, and a chunk record made over it at its new place keeps it. Should the strategy never hear it go (the process stopped between the window's tombstone and the strategy's own writes), its next open, once a host presents, demotes the summary the row's new place leaves non-contiguous instead of refusing the store with `StoreTopologyError`: the summary is dropped with every summary above it, an L1's chunk record is compressed again over its longest run of rows the window holds together, and its other rows are chunked again where the window holds them, with one `[autobiographical] the window holds the leaves of … apart` warning. The demote takes every summary the open's topology audit names once a host presents, whatever made it non-contiguous, and the open throws `StoreTopologyError` only for what the audit still names after it: a store whose crossed ownership predates presentation (#122's cross-era merges, hand surgery) is demoted at its first open after a host presents, not refused, so an operator who wants it kept for a repair opens a copy with `topologyPolicy: 'report'` and `auditOnly: true` (a `'report'` open alone still lets go of what covers a row out of the window, below). The audit runs in the order of the strategy's view: record order in legacy mode, window order once a host presents. This applies under `topologyPolicy` `'reject'`, the default; `'report'` and an audit-only open record the summary and leave it as they find it, and a store in legacy mode is refused as before. `StoreTopologyError`'s message advises an open with `topologyPolicy: 'report'` and `auditOnly: true` to inspect a store it refuses, and says that such an open still writes the load-time repairs of what readers can't use (as every open does) and that a `'report'` open without `auditOnly` also lets go of what covers a row out of the window (only `'reject'` demotes), so a store is inspected as it is only through a copy.
  - A row that left and stays out, which the strategy never heard go (the process stopped between the window's tombstone and the strategy's own writes, or a manager whose strategy has no `onRetracted`, such as `PassthroughStrategy`, took it out), is let go of at the strategy's next open once a host presents, as `onRetracted` lets go of it, with one `[autobiographical] … row(s) out of the window are still folded` warning naming the rows and what changed: it no longer renders inside its summary. The rows are those the window index took out and hasn't presented again (the new optional `WindowIndexView.left()`); a row removed before the window was written down was never in it, and what folded it stays, as in legacy mode. This applies under any `topologyPolicy`; an audit-only open changes nothing.
  - Another strategy instance sharing the store also reads the window before it writes over a row: an L1 or a merge whose span holds a row out of the window (`WindowIndexView.left()`) writes nothing, also where the store's chunk record and summaries don't say so (a `KnowledgeStrategy`, which keeps no chunk records, or an instance whose own writes after the window's tombstone failed), and the instance lets go of the row as its `onRetracted` would have, with a `discarding L1: row(s) … left the window mid-request through another instance sharing the store` or `executeMerge: row(s) … under its sources left the window …` warning; found before a merge's call, it makes none. Before, either was written over the row, which then rendered folded until that instance's next open.
  - A compression that ends with no summary asks the same before it quarantines its span: where another instance sharing the store gave a row of the span up from its chunk record, or the row is out of the window, it writes no `exhausted` record and sends no alert, with an `[autobiographical] the ladder ended with no summary and quarantines nothing: …` warning, and takes the change up as an L1 does. Before, it asked only whether its own instance had heard a row go, and quarantined the obsolete span until the paid-off sweep cleared it.
  - The open's demote also takes an L1, or an uncompressed chunk record, whose rows the window holds together but in another order, as a row presented again right after the rest of them leaves them when the strategy never heard it go: the L1 is dropped, with every summary above it, and its record compressed again over the rows in window order, and such a record holds them in window order, with the `the window holds the leaves of … apart or out of order […]` (or `… uncompressed chunk record(s) apart or out of order`) warning. Before, the audit asked only for holes, so the summary over the rows in their old order survived.
  - `StoreTopologyError`'s message also says that the open that threw it has already written its load-time repairs and, once a host presents, what it demoted and let go of, so an operator who reads it knows the store was changed before the advice to inspect a copy.
  - A removal of a range through the manager (`removeMessages`, or a sharded `addMessage` that fails partway removing what it stored) takes the window's rows out and tells the strategy even where pruning the `holdCompression` holds it ended throws, as `removeMessage` does where the context log's work throws.
  - A sharded `addMessage` that fails partway, whose read of the shards it removes throws, still takes a shard a host's listener presented out of the window, and tells the strategy by the shard's id alone, with `removed: true`, as `removeMessage` tells a row it couldn't read.
  - A call that waited for the strategy's initialization tells a row only where the window of the branch the strategy initialized on held it: where that branch's index took it out, holds it at another ordinal, or holds it where the record no longer does. A branch made before the row was presented never held it, and isn't told.
  - A row of a call that waited, which another manager removed before the call was made, is told by its id alone, with `removed: true`, in a call of its own after the rows of that call the store holds, as `retractRows` tells such a row.
  - `scripts/repair-topology.ts` plans over the namespace's window once a host presents rows to it: the rows presented, in the order they were, as the strategy reads them and the script's verification audits them (the `--messages-state` slot only: rows a manager merges from auxiliary slots aren't in the plan or the verification, as before). Over the record, a summary the window holds apart read as clean.
  - The read of the window by which another strategy instance sharing the store finds a row of an L1's or a merge's span out of it (above) also finds one that left and was presented again through the other instance, which `WindowIndexView.left()` no longer names: an L1, and a merge after its call, compare the span's ordinals with those the window gave its rows when the work began, and each check, a merge's at its start too, finds a row the window holds past every later row of the span, where a presentation puts a row presented again. Either writes nothing, and the instance lets go of the row as its `onRetracted` would have, so its chunks are made again where the window now holds the row, with a `discarding L1: row(s) … left the window and were presented again elsewhere in it mid-request …` or `executeMerge: row(s) … (and […] were presented again elsewhere in it) …` warning. A span the window holds out of order with nothing moved, as the leaves of two summaries whose rows cross are, isn't read as moved: a merge of such sources (kept under `topologyPolicy` `'report'`) is refused by its topology check, and nothing is unfolded. A summary over a row that stands past a run the next summary holds can't be told from a row presented again, and is read as one. Before, a `KnowledgeStrategy`, which keeps no chunk records, wrote the L1 over the rows in their old order.
  - A chunk record that keeps a row presented again, beside a row of its own that didn't leave, holds its rows in window order, a row the view filter hides (`viewFilter`) at its own place in the window, which the window's ordinals give before the filter; so the record's chunk stands in window order, and an L1 over it is written once. The open's demote keeps such a hidden row in the record it trims to its longest run: the view never lists the row, so released it would never be chunked again.
  - A state write that throws while the strategy lets go of a row (the summaries', the chunk records' or the merge queue's) is read back, since a write can land and then throw: `AutobiographicalStrategy` holds what the write put where the store holds it, and what it held before where the store doesn't. Where the store can't be read to tell, it holds what the write put, so it writes nothing back that it let go of; the next open repairs what the store may still hold. Its chunks are rebuilt over the window all the same: the failure is logged as `Strategy onRetracted failed for …`, the writes after it aren't made, and the next open, once a host presents, repairs what they would have done, as after a process stop between them. Before, each was changed in memory before its write, so a write that threw left the strategy's memory apart from the store (an L1 gone from memory and kept in the store, which a later open could keep over a row presented again in its old order), and its chunks still held the row.
