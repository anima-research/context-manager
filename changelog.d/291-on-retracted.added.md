- `ContextStrategy.onRetracted(rows, ctx, { undone, removed, ids })`: the strategy hears rows as they leave the agent's window (agent-framework #291), with the rows that left, in the order the window held them, every shard of a body, and a context whose view no longer holds them (except where a `presentRows` moves a body whole, below: the move is one write, so the view holds the shards that moved at their new places). `ids` names every row that left in the call, in the same order, and `rows` gives the ones the manager can give: a row the store holds, or one a removal through this manager read before it went. A row removed through another manager, which this one doesn't hear, has no row to give: it comes by its id alone, with `removed: true`, and `rows` can be empty, so a strategy that keeps rows by id lets go of it all the same.
  - `retractRows` calls it once per call, after the rows are out and before it returns, with `undone` as given and `removed: false`; the rows the store no longer holds, which it takes out by their ids, are told in a second call of their own, by `ids` alone, with `removed: true`. `retractRemoved` and `retractAbsentRows` call it by `ids` alone, with `removed: true`. Once a host presents rows on a branch, a removal through the manager (`removeMessage`, `removeMessages`) of rows the window held calls it with the rows as they were before they went, `removed: true`, whether or not the window's tombstone could be written, since the removal stands; a row whose tombstone failed is told again, by its id, when `retractAbsentRows` takes it out. A `presentRows` that puts a body back whole, its first shard out and later ones in, calls it for those later shards before `onPresented` hears the body. In legacy mode a removal calls nothing: the window is the record, and a strategy that folded the row keeps what it folded, as before.
  - Fire and forget, as `onPresented` (#288): a throw or a rejection is logged as `Strategy onRetracted failed for …`, naming the rows, and the rows stay out. A call's tombstones are one record (see the window index entry), so a write the store refuses takes nothing out and tells the strategy nothing. While the strategy initializes, the calls wait and are made in order once it has, before the rows presented meanwhile are heard; they wait for the manager's strategy, whether or not the one initializing has the hook, so one set with `setStrategy()` meanwhile is told; a row presented again since is still given, and a row the initialized branch holds at the ordinal it left, in its window and its record (a branch made before it left), or any row while that branch is in legacy mode, is left out.
  - `AutobiographicalStrategy` (and `KnowledgeStrategy`) let go of what covers a row that left, every row `ids` names: each L1 with the row among its sources is dropped, with every summary above it, and a summary the drop leaves without its parent renders and merges again; each chunk record holding the row gives it up and is compressed again over the rows it keeps (one left with none is removed). The summaries, chunk records and merge queue are written one after another, the summaries first, with one `[autobiographical] … row(s) left the window` warning naming what changed. Should the process stop between those writes, the next open repairs what is left: a chunk record whose L1 is gone is compressed again, a queued merge of a dropped summary goes, and, once a host presents, under `topologyPolicy` `'reject'`, a record whose rows the window holds apart is trimmed to its longest run, its other rows chunked again where the window holds them, with one `[autobiographical] the window holds the rows of … uncompressed chunk record(s) apart` warning, so it is not compressed across the window. A compression or merge in flight over the row writes nothing when it lands, also in another strategy instance sharing the store, which doesn't hear the row go: a merge reads the store's summaries before its call and again before it writes, and one with a source the store no longer holds writes nothing (found before the call, it makes none); an L1 reads its chunk record before it writes, and writes nothing unless the store still holds the record uncompressed, over the rows it held when the compression began. Either then takes up the store's state, with an `executeMerge: … source(s) dropped …` or `discarding L1: its chunk record … changed in the store mid-request` warning. So an undone turn's rows no longer render folded after the undo, and a redo, which presents them again at the end of the window, renders each once, raw there until it is folded again, where it rendered twice before (raw, and inside the summary at its old place). A removal through one manager unfolds the row on every reader the host repairs with `retractRemoved` or `retractAbsentRows`, not only on the manager that removed it. The cost is the fold work over those rows, on each reader: the summaries above them are written again. In legacy mode nothing changes. `PassthroughStrategy` and `WindowedPassthroughStrategy` define no `onRetracted`.
  - A row that left and was presented again before the strategy heard it go (both while it initialized) is in the window again when it is told: what covered it at its old place goes all the same, and a chunk record made over it at its new place keeps it. Should the strategy never hear it go (the process stopped between the window's tombstone and the strategy's own writes), its next open, once a host presents, demotes the summary the row's new place leaves non-contiguous instead of refusing the store with `StoreTopologyError`: the summary is dropped with every summary above it, an L1's chunk record is compressed again over its longest run of rows the window holds together, and its other rows are chunked again where the window holds them, with one `[autobiographical] the window holds the leaves of … apart` warning. The demote takes every summary the open's topology audit names once a host presents, whatever made it non-contiguous, and the open throws `StoreTopologyError` only for what the audit still names after it: a store whose crossed ownership predates presentation (#122's cross-era merges, hand surgery) is demoted at its first open after a host presents, not refused, so an operator who wants it kept for a repair opens it with `'report'` first. The audit runs in the order of the strategy's view: record order in legacy mode, window order once a host presents. This applies under `topologyPolicy` `'reject'`, the default; `'report'` and an audit-only open record the summary and leave it as they find it, and a store in legacy mode is refused as before. `StoreTopologyError`'s message advises an open with `topologyPolicy: 'report'` and `auditOnly: true` to inspect a store it refuses, and says that such an open still writes the load-time repairs of what readers can't use (as every open does) and that a `'report'` open without `auditOnly` writes every repair, so a store is inspected as it is only through a copy.
  - A row that left and stays out, which the strategy never heard go (the process stopped between the window's tombstone and the strategy's own writes, or a manager whose strategy has no `onRetracted`, such as `PassthroughStrategy`, took it out), is let go of at the strategy's next open once a host presents, as `onRetracted` lets go of it, with one `[autobiographical] … row(s) out of the window are still folded` warning naming the rows and what changed: it no longer renders inside its summary. The rows are those the window index took out and hasn't presented again (the new optional `WindowIndexView.left()`); a row removed before the window was written down was never in it, and what folded it stays, as in legacy mode. This applies under any `topologyPolicy`; an audit-only open changes nothing.
  - A removal of a range through the manager (`removeMessages`, or a sharded `addMessage` that fails partway removing what it stored) takes the window's rows out and tells the strategy even where pruning the `holdCompression` holds it ended throws, as `removeMessage` does where the context log's work throws.
  - A sharded `addMessage` that fails partway, whose read of the shards it removes throws, still takes a shard a host's listener presented out of the window, and tells the strategy by the shard's id alone, with `removed: true`, as `removeMessage` tells a row it couldn't read.
  - A call that waited for the strategy's initialization tells a row only where the window of the branch the strategy initialized on held it: where that branch's index took it out, holds it at another ordinal, or holds it where the record no longer does. A branch made before the row was presented never held it, and isn't told.
  - A row of a call that waited, which another manager removed before the call was made, is told by its id alone, with `removed: true`, in a call of its own after the rows of that call the store holds, as `retractRows` tells such a row.
